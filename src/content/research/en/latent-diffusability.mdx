## Overview

Visual tokenizers compress images into a representation that a generative model can learn. A useful research question is whether good reconstruction alone tells us enough about that representation.

This page is an **illustrative research brief**. It demonstrates how a research question, method, and evidence could be presented. No experiments or findings are claimed here.

## Background

A latent diffusion pipeline has two distinct stages: an autoencoder maps an image into a latent space, and a generative model learns a distribution in that space. Qualities that help the first stage may not fully describe the difficulty of the second.

For an encoder $E$ and decoder $D$, the reconstruction path is

$$
x \xrightarrow{E} z \xrightarrow{D} \hat{x}.
$$

Reconstruction measures information recovered through $D$. Generative evaluation asks a separate question about samples from the learned distribution. A future study would need to keep these evaluations distinct.

## Approach

A possible investigation would compare representations under a fixed downstream training protocol:

1. Specify the dataset, tokenizer, model, and compute budget.
2. Measure reconstruction and representation properties separately.
3. Train comparable generative models under the same protocol.
4. Examine which representation properties track downstream behavior.
